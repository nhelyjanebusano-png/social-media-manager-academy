WEEK 1

Social Media Manager Foundations

Start from zero: understand what a Social Media Manager actually owns, how platforms differ, and how to build a professional operating system.

LESSON 1

What a Social Media Manager Actually Does

📘 Teaching

A Social Media Manager plans, produces, publishes, monitors, analyzes, and improves social communication. The job is not simply posting every day. You manage attention, community, brand voice, content operations, platform-native execution, reporting, and risk. In some companies you also coordinate creators, paid media, customer care, and launches.

💼 Real Manager Scenario

A founder says, 'Our engagement is down.' You do not immediately post more. You inspect audience, content themes, reach, watch time, saves, comments, traffic, recent changes, and business goals. Then you form a hypothesis and test it.

✍️ Practice

Write the job description you think a professional Social Media Manager should own. Divide it into strategy, production, publishing, community, analytics, monetization, and risk.

🎯 Mastery Standard

You can explain the difference between activity and effective social management.

LESSON 2

The Social Media Funnel

📘 Teaching

A social funnel describes how people move from discovering a brand to engaging, visiting, converting, and potentially becoming repeat customers or advocates. Awareness is not the same as conversion, and a viral view does not automatically equal revenue.

💼 Real Manager Scenario

A recipe Reel may attract 500,000 views but produce few customers. A smaller tutorial may generate 50,000 views and many qualified clicks. The manager needs to understand the business outcome behind the attention.

✍️ Practice

Draw a funnel for a fictional business and assign platform metrics to each stage.

🎯 Mastery Standard

You can connect social metrics to business outcomes rather than chasing vanity numbers.

LESSON 3

Organic vs Paid Social

📘 Teaching

Organic content is distributed without paying for each impression; paid social uses advertising budget to reach selected audiences. They can support each other, but the strategy and measurement are different.

💼 Real Manager Scenario

A strong organic video can become an ad asset, while a paid campaign can reveal audience questions that inspire future organic content.

✍️ Practice

Create an organic-versus-paid decision table for five campaign goals.

🎯 Mastery Standard

You can explain when a problem needs better content, targeting, budget, offer, or some combination.

LESSON 4

Content Pillars and Brand Voice

📘 Teaching

Content pillars are repeatable subject areas that organize what a brand talks about. Brand voice is the personality and communication style expressed consistently across content. Together they create recognizable, repeatable publishing.

💼 Real Manager Scenario

A café might use pillars such as product education, behind the scenes, customer stories, local culture, and offers. The same brand voice should remain recognizable even when formats change.

✍️ Practice

Create five content pillars and a voice guide for a fictional brand.

🎯 Mastery Standard

You can build a content system instead of inventing random posts every day.

LESSON 5

The Social Media Operating System

📘 Teaching

A reliable social team uses an operating system: strategy, content calendar, production workflow, approvals, publishing checklist, community queue, analytics dashboard, and review cadence.

💼 Real Manager Scenario

Without a system, missed approvals, wrong links, duplicate posts, outdated offers, and late campaigns become normal.

✍️ Practice

Draw the end-to-end workflow from idea → brief → production → QA → approval → publish → monitor → report → improve.

🎯 Mastery Standard

You can describe the complete life cycle of a social post.

WEEK 2

Audience, Research, and Strategy

Learn how to understand audiences, competitors, platform behavior, and trends before building content.

LESSON 1

Audience Research

📘 Teaching

Audience research identifies who you are trying to reach, what they care about, what problems they have, what language they use, and where they spend attention. It prevents strategy from becoming guesswork.

💼 Real Manager Scenario

A skincare brand should not treat all followers as the same. A beginner audience may need basic education, while advanced users may want ingredient comparisons.

✍️ Practice

Build three audience personas based on a fictional product, including goals, pain points, objections, content preferences, and buying triggers.

🎯 Mastery Standard

You can turn audience observations into content opportunities.

LESSON 2

Social Listening

📘 Teaching

Social listening means systematically monitoring conversations, comments, mentions, keywords, competitors, customer complaints, and emerging questions. Listening can reveal demand and reputation risks.

💼 Real Manager Scenario

If people repeatedly ask the same question in comments, that question can become a content series or FAQ.

✍️ Practice

Create a listening board with brand mentions, product questions, competitor topics, trends, and risk signals.

🎯 Mastery Standard

You can distinguish useful signals from random noise.

LESSON 3

Competitor Analysis

📘 Teaching

Competitor analysis is not copying. It means studying positioning, formats, posting patterns, offers, engagement patterns, audience comments, strengths, gaps, and opportunities.

💼 Real Manager Scenario

If competitors all publish polished product shots, a brand may find an opportunity in demonstrations, education, founder storytelling, or customer proof.

✍️ Practice

Analyze three fictional competitors and identify five gaps a new brand could own.

🎯 Mastery Standard

You can create strategic opportunities rather than duplicate competitors.

LESSON 4

Trend Research

📘 Teaching

A trend is useful only when it is relevant to the audience and brand. Study search trends, platform discovery surfaces, recurring formats, cultural moments, sounds, topics, and conversation patterns. Avoid using a trend just because it is popular.

💼 Real Manager Scenario

A trending audio can be irrelevant to a B2B company. A trending business question may be a stronger content opportunity even if it has less obvious entertainment value.

✍️ Practice

Create a trend-scoring model using relevance, audience fit, timing, brand fit, effort, risk, and expected value.

🎯 Mastery Standard

You can decide whether a trend deserves action.

LESSON 5

Content Strategy and Objectives

📘 Teaching

A strategy states who you serve, what you want to achieve, what you will publish, where, how often, and how success will be measured. Objectives should connect to business goals.

💼 Real Manager Scenario

If the goal is lead generation, 'get more followers' may be a weak primary objective. You may instead track qualified clicks, leads, conversion rate, and cost per qualified lead.

✍️ Practice

Write a 30-day social strategy for a fictional business with three objectives and five content pillars.

🎯 Mastery Standard

You can explain why every content activity exists.

  1. Platform revenue sharing: eligible ads or platform revenue programs.

SEO 01

What SEO Is and How Search Works

📘 Teaching

Search Engine Optimization (SEO) is the practice of making content easier for search engines to discover, crawl, understand, and appropriately surface. Google describes Search Essentials as the baseline technical requirements, spam policies, and key best practices for Search. SEO can improve visibility, but no legitimate SEO process can guarantee a first-place ranking.

💼 Manager Scenario

A social campaign sends thousands of people to a website, but the pages are hard to find in search. You need enough SEO knowledge to identify whether the problem is content, site structure, technical accessibility, or measurement.

🎯 Mastery Standard

SEO 02

Search Intent and Keyword Research

📘 Teaching

Search intent is the purpose behind a query: learning, finding a specific site or brand, comparing options, or taking an action. Keyword research is the process of understanding the language people use to find solutions. Good SEO starts with the problem and intent, not a list of random high-volume terms.

💼 Manager Scenario

'How to make cassava cake' and 'buy cassava cake Tacloban' represent different intents. One suggests learning; the other suggests purchase intent.

📘 Teaching

On-page SEO makes a page understandable to users and search systems. Learn how to create accurate titles, descriptive meta descriptions, clear headings, useful internal links, descriptive link text, readable URLs, and focused content. 💼 Manager Scenario

A page called 'Our Services' competes with hundreds of vague pages. A more specific, accurate title and structure can communicate the page topic much better.

🧠 Algorithm Lab — Learn How Distribution Systems Work

Do not treat an algorithm as a secret formula with one magic trick. A better model is: eligible content → candidate selection → ranking/recommendation → user feedback → system learning. Platforms use different signals, surfaces, safety rules, and definitions. Your job as a manager is to understand the system, measure outcomes, and test hypotheses.

Golden rule: There is no universal “algorithm.” Study the specific surface: Feed, Reels, For You, Search, Shorts, Home, Explore, Stories, LIVE, etc. Study the platform's official documentation, then validate ideas against your own analytics.

Algorithm Study Method

1. SurfaceWhich feed, search page, recommendation area, or product?
2. SignalsWhat user, content, context, and quality signals are documented?
3. EligibilityWhat content is excluded, limited, or made ineligible?
4. BehaviorWhat do people choose, watch, skip, share, save, click, or reject?
5. MeasurementWhich analytics explain the result?
6. ExperimentWhat one meaningful variable will you test next?

🌍 Global Content Calendar & Seasonal Strategy

This is your annual content-planning engine. It teaches you how to build a social calendar around seasons, global observances, country-specific holidays, cultural moments, commercial events, and your own brand campaigns without turning the feed into a wall of generic holiday posts.

Professional mindset: “Worldwide” does not mean one universal calendar. It means you know how to build a global master calendar and then localize it by market.

Seasonal creative should be localized. A July “summer essentials” campaign may be logical in Europe or North America but inappropriate in Australia or South Africa.

🎯 The Global Holiday Content Framework

Before adding a holiday to a content calendar, ask six questions:

1. RelevanceDoes this matter to our audience?
2. AuthenticityDo we have a real reason to participate?
3. GeographyWhich countries actually observe it?
4. TimingIs the date fixed, movable, lunar, or locally determined?
5. SensitivityCould the post be disrespectful, trivializing, or overly commercial?
6. ObjectiveIs this awareness, community, education, traffic, leads, sales, or retention?
Do not create a “holiday dump.” A professional calendar prioritizes relevant observances, then adds local national and commercial dates for the specific audience. A global brand should never assume one holiday or season fits everyone.

🗓️ Build a 12-Month Global Social Calendar

Step 1: Start with permanent brand campaigns and launches.

Step 2: Add platform seasons and audience behavior.

Step 3: Add relevant global observances from authoritative sources.

Step 4: Add country-specific national/public holidays.

Step 5: Add cultural/religious observances only for markets and audiences where they are relevant.

Step 6: Add commercial moments such as shopping events, sales periods, or industry events.

Step 7: Assign a content angle, platform, owner, asset type, CTA, approval gate, and measurement.
